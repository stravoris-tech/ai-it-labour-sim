# Model guide

The menu of questions `ai-it-labour-sim` can answer: the scenarios, the levers and the main outputs. It is generated by `tools/model_guide.py` from `src/aisim/scenarios.py`, `docs/model/inventory.csv`, `src/aisim/data/model_docs.csv` and `tools/guide_outputs.csv`; do not edit it by hand.

## 1. How to ask the model a question

A question is a model, a scenario or lever change, and the outputs to read.

- **Choose a model.** `phase9` is the default and fits every question. For a question that depends on how fast AI adoption spreads after 2026, run `phase9_highadoption` alongside it on the same draws and report both: the 2024-2026 data identify the early adoption path but not its later speed (README, Models).
- **Choose a scenario or a lever change.** Name a scenario from section 2, set levers from section 3 as `overrides`, or sample them for a Monte Carlo. With `perturb_from=Y` the calibrated baseline runs unchanged until Y and the changes act from Y; validation rejects a change that cannot act from Y (at Y = 2026.5: Visa crackdown).
- **Choose outputs.** Name them from `aisim.catalogue()`; section 4 lists the main ones.
- **Read the answer as a projection.** The model gives scenario projections, not forecasts. Read every result as "under these assumptions" (README).

This example runs Fast AI on `phase9` from mid-2026 and prints GCC headcount:

```python
from aisim import Experiment, run_experiment


def main() -> None:
    exp = Experiment(model="phase9", scenario="Fast AI", perturb_from=2026.5)
    res = run_experiment(exp, outputs=["HeadTotal", "Head_GCC"])
    print(res.bands("Head_GCC", years=(2026, 2040)))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

## 2. Scenarios

The 14 scenarios of `aisim.SCENARIOS` and the 1 stress set of `aisim.STRESS_SCENARIOS`, by the name `Experiment(scenario=...)` and `run_scenarios` take. Each sets the levers shown, from `src/aisim/scenarios.py`; every other parameter keeps its Base value.

| Scenario | What it changes | What it represents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Base` | nothing: the calibrated baseline | The calibrated baseline, with no lever changed. |
| `Slow AI` | `g` 0.05; `VerifDecline` 0 | AI capability grows more slowly and checking AI output never gets cheaper. |
| `Fast AI` | `g` 0.2; `VerifDecline` 0.1 | AI capability grows faster and checking AI output gets cheaper faster. |
| `Strict regulation` | `RegScenario` 1.5 | Regulation slows clients' adoption of AI more than in Base. |
| `Light regulation` | `RegScenario` 0.5 | Regulation slows clients' adoption of AI less than in Base. |
| `Compute shock` | `AIShockMult` 3; `AIShockStart` 2027; `AIShockEnd` 2029.5 | A temporary shortage of AI compute makes AI work dearer for a few years. |
| `Visa crackdown` | `VTEnd` 0.8; `VTRampStart` 2025.7; `VTRampEnd` 2027.7 | Onshore work visas become much harder to get, with tightening starting earlier and reaching a higher level than in Base. |
| `Visa relaxation` | `VTEnd` 0.1; `VTRampStart` 2027; `VTRampEnd` 2028 | Onshore work visas become easier to get: tightening ends lower and sooner than in Base. |
| `Downturn` | `BudgetShock` 0.15; `BudgetShockStart` 2027; `BudgetShockEnd` 2029 | A temporary downturn cuts the growth of clients' IT budgets for a few years. |
| `Jevons rebound` | `JevonsEps` 0.5 | Cheaper IT work draws out more demand for applications and data work. |
| `Demand stalls` | `DemandGrowthLate` 0 | Underlying IT demand stops growing after mid-2026. |
| `Demand boom` | `DemandGrowthLate` 0.05 | Underlying IT demand grows faster after mid-2026. |
| `GCC wave persists` | `GCCAppealKeep` 1 | Clients keep their full appetite for captive centres in India after the wave peaks. |
| `GCC wave fades` | `GCCAppealKeep` 0 | Clients' appetite for captive centres in India fades away after the wave peaks. |
| `Fast AI + OIT price shock` | `g` 0.2; `VerifDecline` 0.1; `PriceShock_OIT` 0.2, not a lever: a stress-test input (inventory lever = no) | Fast AI, plus the offshore Indian IT vendor losing a share of its revenue from the price-shock year onward: a stress test in which every phase9 term acts, the loops GCC scale learning (R7), capacity exit (C2), client flight (C4), attrition cost (R10) and skill erosion (R4), and the price floor (C5) and AI-discount ceiling (C6) rules. |

## 3. Levers

The 31 levers of `docs/model/inventory.csv` (its `lever` column begins with "yes"), 23 scenario and tornado levers and 8 structural loop strengths of `phase9`, in inventory order. Each is a single value; set it in `overrides` or sample it.

| Lever | What it is | Units | Base value | Scenarios that move it | Sensitivity range (tornado_levers) | At year Y |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `DemandGrowthLate` Late demand growth rate | The underlying yearly growth rate of IT demand after LateYear; empty means the same as DemandGrowth. | fraction per year | same as `DemandGrowth` (0.03) | Demand stalls: 0; Demand boom: 0.05 | 0.015 to 0.045 | - |
| `g` Capability growth rate | The fraction of the remaining gap between each work type's automatable share and its ceiling that AI capability closes each year. | fraction per year | 0.1 | Slow AI: 0.05; Fast AI: 0.2; Fast AI + OIT price shock: 0.2 | 0.06 to 0.15 | builds gradually |
| `Verif` Initial verification overhead | The 2024 effort spent checking AI output per unit of automated work, which then falls at VerifDecline a year. | dimensionless | 0.544428 (phase9_highadoption 0.521413) | - | 0.381099 to 0.707756 (phase9_highadoption 0.364989 to 0.677837) | cannot act from Y (a 2024 level) |
| `VerifDecline` Verification decline rate | The fraction of the verification overhead that disappears each year as AI models become more reliable. | fraction per year | 0.05 | Slow AI: 0; Fast AI: 0.1; Fast AI + OIT price shock: 0.1 | 0 to 0.1 | builds gradually |
| `JuniorCut` Junior hiring cut | The cut in the junior share of staff per unit of delivery AI adoption above its 2024 level. | fraction | 0.761126 (phase9_highadoption 0.663416) | - | 0.380563 to 1.14169 (phase9_highadoption 0.331708 to 0.995124) | steps |
| `R6e` Capacity exit strength | The strength of loop C2: the fraction of a vendor's desired staff cut per margin point of relative-viability shortfall below the R6 threshold. | fraction per margin point | 0.005 | - | 0.002 to 0.012 | - |
| `VTEnd` End visa tightness | Visa tightness after the ramp, on a scale from 0 open to 1 closed: the visa policy scenario lever. | dimensionless | 0.3 | Visa crackdown: 0.8; Visa relaxation: 0.1 | - | - |
| `VTRampEnd` Visa ramp end | The calendar year in which the visa tightness ramp ends. | year | 2031 | Visa crackdown: 2027.7; Visa relaxation: 2028 | - | - |
| `VTRampStart` Visa ramp start | The calendar year in which the visa tightness ramp starts. | year | 2027 | Visa crackdown: 2025.7; Visa relaxation: 2027 | - | - |
| `EnrolSensitivity` Enrolment sensitivity | The sensitivity of enrolment to student-perceived unemployment above its normal rate. | dimensionless | 1 | - | 0.5 to 1.5 | - |
| `R4a` Onshore skill erosion | The strength of R4 skill erosion onshore: the largest proportional rise in the seeker exit rate for long spells, relative to 2024; 0 turns it off. | dimensionless | 0.1 | - | 0 to 0.25 | - |
| `AICostDecline` AI cost decline | The yearly fractional fall in AI cost per unit of work, which also drives the fall in consumer credit cost. | fraction per year | 0.15 | - | 0.08 to 0.25 | builds gradually |
| `AIDiscCeil` AI discount ceiling | The largest AI discount clients can extract on new contract prices: the price anchor cannot fall below one minus it (loop R6, C6); 0 turns it off. | fraction | 0.3 | - | 0.2 to 0.45 | - |
| `AIShockEnd` AI shock end | The calendar year the AI cost shock window closes; the shock no longer applies from this year on. | year | 2032 | Compute shock: 2029.5 | - | - |
| `AIShockMult` AI cost shock multiplier | A test lever multiplying AI cost per unit inside the AI cost shock window, 1 for no shock. | multiplier | 1 | Compute shock: 3 | - | - |
| `AIShockStart` AI shock start | The calendar year the AI cost shock window opens; the shock applies from this year up to, but not including, its end year. | year | 2030 | Compute shock: 2027 | - | - |
| `PriceFloor` New-bid price floor | The floor on each new-bid price as a multiple of the vendor's expected cost per unit of work (loop R6); 0 turns it off. | multiplier | 1 | - | 0.85 to 1.08 | - |
| `PriceHorizon` Client price horizon | The years of client-perceived productivity trend that clients price into the anchor for new contract prices. | years | 6.99998 (phase9_highadoption 7) | - | 4 to 8 | steps |
| `R10rc` Attrition replacement cost | The years of wage a vendor pays to replace each voluntary exit above its 2024 attrition rate (loop R10); 0 turns it off. | years | 0.1816 | - | 0.0748 to 0.3223 | - |
| `R6Theta` Viability stress threshold | The relative-viability threshold, as a fraction of the normal margin, below which vendor capacity exit and client flight begin (loop R6). | fraction | 0.8 | - | 0.65 to 0.92 | - |
| `kR7` GCC scale learning strength | The strength of loop R7: the logit gain in every sector's GCC value per unit of log growth in the economy-wide GCC work share since 2024. | logit units | 0.4 | - | 0.15 to 0.75 | steps |
| `kViab` Client flight strength | The strength of loop C4: the sourcing value a vendor loses per unit of relative-viability shortfall not yet habituated. | logit units | 5.8562 | - | 0 to 10.6511 | - |
| `SrcCostSens` Sourcing cost sensitivity | The sourcing value lost per unit of an option's cost relative to the reference cost. | logit units | 3 | - | 2 to 4 | steps |
| `TMBrake` T&M brake strength | How much a vendor's AI adoption speed falls per unit of effort-billed share of its contract book, the strength of the T&M paradox. | dimensionless | 0.4 | - | 0.2 to 0.6 | - |
| `aL_max` Max LOB automatable share | The ceiling that the automatable share of line-of-business work approaches over time. | fraction | 0.35 | - | 0.25 to 0.45 | builds gradually |
| `BudgetShock` Budget shock | The fractional cut in IT budget growth during the budget shock window, a downturn lever. | fraction | 0 | Downturn: 0.15 | - | - |
| `BudgetShockEnd` Budget shock end | The year the budget shock ends, excluded from the shock window. | year | 2032 | Downturn: 2029 | - | - |
| `BudgetShockStart` Budget shock start | The year the budget shock begins. | year | 2030 | Downturn: 2027 | - | - |
| `GCCAppealKeep` GCC appeal kept | The share of the peak GCC appeal that remains after the fade; 1 keeps the whole wave. | multiplier | 0.5 | GCC wave persists: 1; GCC wave fades: 0 | 0 to 1 | - |
| `JevonsEps` Jevons elasticity | The elasticity of app and data demand to the perceived cost of IT work, the strength of the Jevons rebound; 0 switches it off. | dimensionless | 0 | Jevons rebound: 0.5 | 0 to 0.5 | steps |
| `RegScenario` Regulation scenario | The scenario multiplier on regulatory drag: 0 light-touch, 1 base, 1.5 strict. | multiplier | 1 | Strict regulation: 1.5; Light regulation: 0.5 | 0.5 to 1.5 | - |

- **Base value:** the `phase9` value, with the `phase9_highadoption` value where it differs; fitted values to 6 significant figures.
- **Sensitivity range (tornado_levers):** from `aisim.scenarios.tornado_levers(model=...)`, the one source of record for lever ranges: the 14 tornado ranges and the structural loop strengths' evidence ranges. `run_experiment` samples only the levers an experiment names, so a Monte Carlo samples these ranges when it passes them. "-": no range. The set depends on the perturb year: from 2026.5 it drops `Verif`; from 2027 it drops `DemandGrowthLate`, `Verif`.
- **At year Y:** what the lever does when it acts from a perturb year Y, as the README records it ("Perturb from year Y"): it "steps" at Y, or it "builds gradually" from its base path. "-": not recorded in the README or the Q05 analysis.

**Stress-test input (not a lever).** `PriceShock` Price shock (fraction), per vendor, see inventory.csv: phase9 Base 0 for every vendor. `STRESS_SCENARIOS` sets it: 'Fast AI + OIT price shock' sets `PriceShock_OIT` 0.2.

## 4. Main outputs

18 outputs from `tools/guide_outputs.csv`, with names, labels and units from `src/aisim/data/model_docs.csv`. A name with a dimension in braces gives every member, labelled as in the specification. Values are phase9 Base: one deterministic run, recorded every step, rounded to 3 significant figures. Shares and rates are in percent. Headcounts are in model units, which are proportions, not national counts: compare changes and shares, not absolute levels (specification 07, "Model units are proportions, not national counts"). Work is in BFY, a unit of IT work: one person-year of 2024 delivery (`src/aisim/scalar/capability.py:77-81`, `src/aisim/scalar/work.py:70`; `tools/model_docs.py:972`). Money is in real USD a year.
